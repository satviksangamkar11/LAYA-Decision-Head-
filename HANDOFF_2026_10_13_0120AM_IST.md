### The experiment (user GO, 13 October: four arms, 5-fold cross-validation, Kaggle GPU)

| Arm | What | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| A | reference: Bridge A frozen, all 31 head tensors at 2e-5, 300 steps, batch 8, unknown weight 0.5 | must reproduce the recorded 54.3% |
| B | calibration control, NO training: one temperature fitted on the fit pools for the released head | loss and calibration error only; top-1 cannot change |
| B+ | **added by the agent, disclosed:** A started from the temperature-scaled scorer, same rates | without it, C differs from A in two things at once |
| C | B+ with a higher rate on context layer 1 (`ctx.layers.0.*`): 1e-4 and 2e-4, the other 23 tensors at 2e-5 | can layer 1 adapt? The rate is chosen on the inner TRAIN development folds |
| D | C (chosen rate) + native replay: 8 native TRAIN items per step, weight 1.0 | does replay prevent forgetting? |

- **Replay target:** the ORIGINAL model's predicted distribution over the options (KL), i.e. "keep behaving as released". Not the gold labels. Gold labels are used
  only to report accuracy on the 600 native validation items, which are never trained on. (All 2,988 native training items do have a gold label.)
- **Reproduction gate (stop if it fails):** arm A in the reference configuration (fit folds 1-4, development fold 0, seeds 0-2) on the GPU must give three-seed means
  within 4.4 points on cleaned VAL (reference 54.35%), 4 points on the development fold (48.72%), 3 points on native (69.06%) and 4 points on the fit pools (55.11%).
  Seed 0 is also repeated on the CPU and compared pool by pool with the recorded run (information only).
- **Primary evaluation:** 5-fold cross-validation on the 303 TRAIN pools (trajectory-disjoint folds; test fold k, development fold (k+1) mod 5, three folds fitted);
  per pool the mean over three seeds; paired difference against A with a trajectory bootstrap.
- **Additional:** each arm in the reference configuration, 3 seeds, on cleaned VAL (with and without context) and the 600 native items; the linear probe out of fold
  and on VAL.
- **Acceptance rule (a practical gate; the 66% floor is a tolerance, not a statistical threshold):** an arm is better than A only if its paired out-of-fold
  difference has an interval above 0 AND its native accuracy stays at or above 66%.
- **Reading fixed beforehand (user):** C beats A with native retained: better optimisation may unlock more. C helps Fable but hurts native and D repairs it: replay
  is valuable. C and D fail to beat A: stop recipe tuning and look at how the head uses the representations. Large seed variation: not enough evidence.
- **Found on the fit pools while preparing the run:** the released head's loss falls all the way to the equal-score value as the temperature grows (negative
  log-likelihood 1.504 at temperature 1, 1.2525 at 20, 1.2517 at 1,000, which equals equal scores). The fitted temperature therefore has no finite optimum.
  The search range is capped at 20 (the agent's choice, written into the plan before the run). "Calibration" here means the scores are flattened almost completely.
- The frozen trainer has one learning rate for all 31 tensors and runs on the CPU only, so the script carries its own loop that mirrors it (same sampling,
  schedule, clipping and checkpoint rule). No head checkpoint is saved by this run; nothing is adopted automatically.
- A 10-step smoke test on a small subset ran on the PC to catch errors before the upload.

## 6. What the results mean

1. The head with a trained adapter generalises (79.2% on unseen trajectories) and uses the context (15 points lost without it). Most of that learning sits in
   the adapter, which only fits Qwen.
2. Training the head alone through a fixed bridge reaches about 54%; the linear probe on the same inputs reads 87 to 89%.
3. The bridge is not the limit. The probe falls inside context layer 1: 87.0% at its input, 76.1% after its attention step, 56.5% after its feed-forward step,
   which writes an update about 96 times larger than what it receives.
4. Attention mixing is not the cause: blocking it changes nothing on Fable pools and costs under 3 points of native accuracy.
5. **Corrected:** the earlier "options collapse, cosine 0.97" is mostly one common vector shared by all options (0.48 with it removed; 0.29 at the scorer input;
   the original model on its own items 0.18). The geometry is not collapsed; the content is poor.
6. Reducing the feed-forward update makes the signal readable again after layer 1 (up to 76.1%) and partly at the scorer input (up to 69.6%), but the head's own
   choices do not improve at any scale, and the same update carries the head's native skill (native falls to 43-47% when it is cut to 3% or less).
   So it cannot simply be weakened. Caution: scaling takes layer 2 and the scorer outside what they were trained on; this does not show training cannot fix it.
7. The released head starts worse than equal scores on Fable pools (loss 1.064 against 0.813) and its best temperature is unbounded. Head-only training spent
   much of its 300 steps flattening the scores (spread 0.73 to 0.28; loss 0.751). The unknown weight is not the problem.
8. A coarse sequence per option does not fix the layer-1 drop.
9. The linear probe is ahead everywhere but never picks an equivalent alternative, so part of its lead is recognising Fable's wording.
10. Nothing shows better coding outcomes. The A5 benchmark was not run.
